US wants Ukraine’s strikes closer to the front. Ukraine says its Starshield link is capped

The Pentagon’s Elbridge Colby repeated his 17 November pitch to allies, while Militarnyi reports the Starshield limits remain unresolved.

byTaisiia Vivdych
23/11/2026

Washington wants its support for Ukraine, "with Europe in the lead," to focus on holding the front lines and on "related mid-range strikes," US Under Secretary of War for Policy Elbridge Colby wrote on 22 November in a thread on X.

However, Starshield, the military version of SpaceX's Starlink, is used, among other things, to control mid-range strike drones, and its restrictions remain unresolved, Ukrainian outlet Militarnyi reported on 23 November. Ukraine says the system has been capped along the front since before 12 November. Colby's nine-post thread did not mention Starshield.

A repeated message

At NATO headquarters in Brussels on 17 November, Colby argued that deep strikes inside Russia had not helped end the war, AP reported; German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius called that criticism "quite a stupid thing."

Ukraine's Defense Ministry puts middle-strike targets roughly 30–200 km away; asked afterward about Starshield, Colby declined to comment, according to AP.

Kyiv reports a Starshield cap, SpaceX denies any change

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy wrote on 12 November that units had logged a 161 km/h Starshield speed limit along the front, making Russian jet drones harder to intercept.

Explore further

The Kerch Bridge span fell without Starlink, a Ukrainian officer says. Only weeks earlier Kyiv was asking Washington for Starshield to strike deep, Rubio said

SpaceX told Axios there was "no change"; Zelenskyy said the company called it a technical problem, a view Kyiv does not share, Interfax-Ukraine reported.

The Foundation for Defense of Democracies said the cap hampers mid-range strikes on moving targets, not deep strikes; after a similar October cap, Defense Minister Yevhenii Khmara said speeds above 400 km/h were back.
