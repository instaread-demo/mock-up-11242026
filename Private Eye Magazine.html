AGREEMENTS galore are on the menu at the love-in that will be the EU-UK summit on 20 November. Anxious for another PR coup, Andy Burnham’s government wants to prove it is getting the EU “reset” Keir Starmer promised.

Most of the low-hanging fruit, however, has already been picked. An agreement aligning the EU and UK carbon emissions trading schemes was the easiest since the UK emissions trading scheme was almost identical to that of the EU. The deal would enable carbon permits to be traded across the two emissions systems.

Carbon crunch
Linking the trading systems could, the column says, exempt each side from the other’s carbon taxes; the UK border levy, due early next year, mirrors the EU’s.

UK steel exporters would save millions, and a phytosanitary pact on food and drink checks has been trailed.

The column credits the easy progress to Johnson and Sunak-era laws that copied EU rules.

Brussels blitz
Jonathan Reynolds and Hamish Falconer lobbied in Brussels for UK access to Made in Europe and the EU electricity market.

Whitehall has offered about €500m a year for EU students under youth mobility; Made in Europe would make EU firms buy up to 60 percent of their inputs in the bloc.

Germany and other states want UK cohesion fund payments, and the column expects access to cost far more than £450m.
